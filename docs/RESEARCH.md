# Market Research: Faceless Kids & Family Video Channel

_Researched 2026-11-27. Figures marked "third-party" come from analytics vendors or blogs, not from YouTube or TikTok. Use them for direction, not for exact numbers._

---

## TL;DR

1. **Strict "made for kids" content (preschool and early elementary) has the most views and the least money.** Kids & Teens has the lowest median long-form RPM in AIR's 300-channel dataset: **$0.33**. Education & Science has the highest: **$10.22** median, and **$15–18** in a second 274-channel study. Videos set as made for kids can't show personalized ads, and they lose comments, the notification bell and end screens. YouTube is also under organised pressure from more than 135 child-advocacy groups to ban AI-generated kids content, and it says it already limits AI content in the YouTube Kids app to "a small set of high-quality channels."
2. **TikTok has no real kids market to monetize.** The platform is 13+. US under-13s are placed in a restricted, curated experience, and the Creator Rewards Program pays only on original videos **longer than 1 minute**.
3. **YouTube changes the rules on Feb 1, 2027, about 9 weeks from now.**
   - New YPP applicants will need **8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days**. That doubles today's bar.
   - Channels already in YPP will need **10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days** to earn any Shorts revenue.
   - For a new channel, Shorts are a growth engine. Long-form watch hours are the path to monetization.
4. **YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy (clarified July 16, 2026) demonetizes whole channels** that look templated or mass-produced, chase views with distressing content, or use AI "expert" personas. A faceless AI channel only survives if each video has real variation, a narrative arc and accurate information.
5. **Recommendation:** a **general-audience, family-friendly curiosity-science channel**. It is built for curious 9–14 year-olds, but it is written and marketed so that parents, teens and adults enjoy it too. Think Kurzgesagt or Zack D. Films energy, kid-safe, without the dark topics.
   - It earns Education-tier ad rates, keeps comments and notifications, and qualifies for TikTok Creator Rewards.
   - It is honest content, not slop.
   - Format: Shorts plus 61–75s TikTok cuts for discovery, and a weekly 8–12 minute YouTube deep-dive for watch hours and real revenue.

## 1. Who wins in kids and family video right now

### Biggest kids channels (subscribers, Nov 2026)
| Channel | Subs | Format / style | Audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cocomelon – Nursery Rhymes | ~203M (vidIQ, Nov 4 2026) | 3D CG, soft rounded big-eyed characters, saturated primary colors, songs | 1–5, made for kids |
| Vlad and Niki | ~151M | Live action, toys and play | 3–8, made for kids |
| Kids Diana Show / Like Nastya | top 10 globally | Live action, family vlogs and pretend play | 3–8, made for kids |
| Pinkfong (Baby Shark) | ~69M+ (listicle figure) | 2D flat, bright primaries, songs | 1–5, made for kids |

**Common traits:** music and repetition, very saturated colors, big-headed rounded characters, and huge back catalogs of 30–60 minute compilations. These channels win on volume and watch time, not on RPM. Most are studios with licensing and merch deals, such as Cocomelon licensing to Netflix and toy lines. The YouTube ads are a funnel for that business.

### The format closest to what we want: curiosity and science explainers
| Channel | Size | Format | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zack D. Films** | ~29.8M subs, ~84B views, ~19.7M avg views per video (third-party, Nov 2026) | 3D-animated narrated explainers with rapid visuals and curiosity hooks; recent uploads ~9 min | Proves faceless 3D or animated "did you know" content can reach massive scale. Estimated audience: 13–17 at 20%, 18–24 at 40% (third-party). It is **general audience**, not made for kids. |
| Kurzgesagt | ~25M | Flat-vector 2D, saturated neon-on-dark palette, recurring bird characters | General audience. Shows that animated is not the same as made for kids. |
| SciShow Kids, Crash Course Kids, PBS Kids | mid-size | Presenter plus illustrations; "why?" questions | The classic educational-for-kids format. Trusted by parents and teachers, but low RPM because they are made for kids. |
| Peekaboo Kidz (Dr. Binocs), Learn Bright | large | 2D cartoon host explaining science | Made for kids. Long compilations. |

**Zack D. Films title patterns (third-party analysis):** a number or specific noun, plus a hidden or failure hook, plus stakes. Examples: "Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake" and "The Disturbing Story Behind the Starbucks Logo." Its most-viewed topics skew toward disasters, corporate scandals and creepy fiction. **We take the hook mechanics and leave the dark subject matter.** YouTube's teen principles and the July 2026 "off-putting content" category both penalize fear-bait.

### What Gen Alpha is watching (2025–2026)
- YouTube dominates kids' viewing and is forecast to pass linear TV in 2026 (eMarketer). Over half of kids watch YouTube on a TV screen, so a long-form deep-dive can work as "family TV."
- Shorts usage among kids rose to 53%, up 14% year over year (PARK survey via Tubefilter).
- Co-viewed genres: cartoons 48%, funny videos 37%, toys and games 34%, family 32%, music 31% (Precisify, Feb 2026).
- "Brainrot" (Italian brainrot, Steal a Brainrot, phonk-scored overstimulation) and Roblox were the defining kid trends of 2025–26. **We should not make brainrot.** It is exactly the "hard to follow / nonsensical / mass-produced" content YouTube's kids quality principles demonetize. It is useful only as a signal for which characters, music energy and slang kids recognize.

## 2. Money: what each path actually pays

### YouTube
| Item | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form median RPM, Education & Science | $10.22 (300 ch.), $14.97–18.23 (274 ch.) | AIR Media-Tech, third-party |
| Long-form median RPM, Kids & Teens | $0.33; large kids channels $0.49 | AIR, third-party |
| Made-for-kids RPM range | $1–3 vs $5–15 for comparable general-audience content (50–80% lower) | TechTimes, Jul 2026 (unsourced estimate) |
| Kids share of YouTube views vs ad revenue | ~15% of views, ~2% of ~$40B ad revenue | Animaj CEO via MIPblog |
| Shorts RPM, most niches | $0.07–0.20 per 1k; US-heavy audiences ~$0.33 | AIR, third-party |
| Kids Shorts monetized playback rate | 45.7% vs 12.1% for kids long-form (made-for-kids long-form rarely gets ads) | AIR, low-confidence sample |
| Implied Education Shorts RPM | ~$0.54–1.44 (estimate, not measured) | AIR |

**YPP thresholds.** Source: YouTube Help and the YouTube blog, Aug 10, 2026.
- **Today:** 1,000 subs plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed views do not count toward watch hours.
- **New applicants from Feb 1, 2027:** 8,000 watch hours in 365 days, or 20M Shorts views in 90 days.
- **Shorts revenue from Feb 1, 2027:** you need at least 10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days to earn a Shorts revenue share that month. Below that you still earn from long-form.

**What this means for us:** long-form is not optional. 8,000 hours is about 80,000 views of a 10-minute video watched for 6 minutes on average.

### TikTok
- **Creator Rewards Program:** 18+, 10k followers, 100k views in the last 30 days, original videos **longer than 1 minute**. Photo mode and slideshows are excluded.
- **Reported RPM:** about $0.40–1.00+ per 1,000 qualified views (third-party creator reports; TikTok publishes no rate).
- **Audience:** 13+. Under-13s in the US get "TikTok for Younger Users," a curated, restricted feed.

### Beyond ad revenue (usually bigger for education channels)
- Sponsorships: education and edtech brands buy into curiosity channels.
- Affiliate links in descriptions for science kits, telescopes and books.
- An email list of parents and teachers built from free printable worksheets for each video.
- Licensing the back catalog later.

## 3. Policy guardrails this channel must be built around

1. **Made for kids is a legal designation (COPPA), not a style choice.** YouTube's own factors include: subject matter, intended audience in the metadata, child actors, **animated characters or toys that appeal to children**, language aimed at children, early-education songs and play-acting, and advertising to children. Mislabeling child-directed content is a serious violation; Disney paid a $10M COPPA settlement over YouTube mislabeling in Dec 2025. A **general audience** channel must actually be general audience. That means:
   - no "for kids" in titles, tags or descriptions;
   - no toddler mascots or nursery music;
   - narration pitched at teens and adults;
   - topics adults would also click.

   If a video drifts toward under-13-directed content, the QA agent must flag it as made for kids.
2. **Kids & family quality principles.**
   - High quality: curiosity, learning, creativity, life skills, showing the world. This content is boosted in recommendations.
   - Low quality: heavily commercial, negative behaviors, **"deceptively educational" (incorrect info)**, **"hard to follow… often the result of mass production or autogeneration,"** sensational or misleading, strange use of kids' characters. This content can be demonetized, or the channel suspended from YPP.
3. **Teen quality principles** (new teen-content page): avoid narrow body standards, dangerous acts, bullying, wealth obsession and aggression.
4. **Inauthentic content** (renamed July 2025; clarified July 16, 2026). These videos are not monetizable:
   - **(a)** generic or repetitive content that "looks like it's made with a template," such as image slideshows, templated storylines, or characters in the same situation over and over;
   - **(b)** off-putting or distressing content made to chase views, such as animals in peril or shock clips;
   - **(c)** AI personas posing as experts on health, finance or law.

   This is assessed **channel-wide**. Halprin (YouTube Trust & Safety): AI is fine when videos have a "narrative arc" and "show your creativity."
5. **AI disclosure.**
   - YouTube requires disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic media. The API flag is `status.containsSyntheticMedia`.
   - YouTube is building separate AI labels for YouTube Kids.
   - TikTok requires an AI-generated content label on realistic AI content.
   - Stylized animation is lower-risk, but we will disclose whenever imagery is realistic.

## 4. Creative common denominators (what to copy, structurally)

| Element | Pattern in top performers | Our rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook (0–2s)** | No intro cards, logos or "hey guys"; the first frame shows the payoff or the question in motion. The most common retention cliff is a slow open. | The first spoken line *is* the question, over a moving visual. Run a sound-off check on the first 3 seconds. |
| **Length** | Shorts: test 15–35s, then use retention data. Loops (last frame flows into the first) boost replays. TikTok rewards need >60s. Long-form 8–12 min (mid-rolls start at 8 min). | Shorts 35–50s with a looping last line; TikTok 61–75s cut with a bonus fact; long-form 8–12 min with chapters. |
| **Narrative** | Question → surprising answer → 2–3 escalating visual beats → "but here's the crazy part" → payoff. | Mandatory beat structure in the script template, with variation in format and pacing to avoid looking templated. |
| **Colors** | Kids and young audiences respond to bright, saturated, contrasting colors. Cocomelon and Pinkfong use primaries; Kurzgesagt uses neon on dark. Muted thumbnails underperform. | Own brand palette: saturated complementary pairs on a deep background. Same palette every video so the feed recognizes us. |
| **Thumbnails (long-form)** | One big subject, at most 3 words, heavy outline or contrast, faces or expressive characters if used. Keep key elements away from the edges because mobile crops. YouTube advises limiting ALL CAPS and emoji. | Template system: one hero object, 2–3 words, brand color frame. Test variants in YouTube Studio's Test & Compare. |
| **Titles** | Specific number or noun + curiosity gap + stakes ("Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake"). | "What If You Fell Into Jupiter?", "Your Body Does THIS Every Night", with no "for kids" keywords. |
| **Captions** | Big, bold, word-by-word highlighted captions in the center safe zone. | Burned-in kinetic captions synced from word timestamps. |
| **Animation style** | 3D CG (Cocomelon, Zack D.), flat 2D vector (Kurzgesagt, Pinkfong), simple geometric (Numberblocks). | 2.5D flat-vector illustrated scenes, animated in code (parallax, zooms, labeled diagrams, kinetic text). Cheap, consistent, ownable, and not uncanny. |

## 5. Niche options compared

| Option | Ad RPM | TikTok eligible | Policy risk | "Not slop" | Speed to money | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Preschool songs and nursery (made for kids) | $0.3–3 | No (13+) | High: AI kids content under fire; YouTube Kids AI limits | Low | Slow; needs huge volume | ❌ |
| B. Kids science explainers labeled made for kids | $0.3–3 | Weak | Medium | High | Slow | ⚠️ Viable second channel later |
| **C. Family-friendly curiosity science (general audience, 9–14 + adults)** | **$4–15 long-form; Shorts $0.1–0.5** | **Yes** | **Low–medium (must stay accurate and varied)** | **High** | **Medium** | ✅ **Recommended** |
| D. Brainrot and meme characters | Low | Yes | High (inauthentic, low-quality) | None | Fast views, no money | ❌ |
| E. Dark curiosity (disasters, crime) à la Zack D. | High | Yes | Medium (off-putting category); not kid-safe | Medium | Medium | ❌ Not our brand |

**Content pillars for option C:**
1. **What If…?** "What if the Moon disappeared?", "What if you swallowed a magnet?" (the latter answered safely)
2. **How It Works.** Microwaves, noise-cancelling headphones, how a plane stays up.
3. **Animal Superpowers.** Mantis shrimp punch, tardigrades, octopus brains.
4. **Your Amazing Body.** Why we yawn, what happens when you sleep.
5. **Scale & Space.** "How big is the biggest star?", "How deep is the ocean?"
6. **Myth Busted.** "Do goldfish really have a 3-second memory?"
